How the simulation works

  • Each trade risks your risk % of the current balance: a win adds (reward-to-risk × risk %) of the current balance, a loss removes risk % of the current balance. Balances are checked after every trade.
  • A trading day is exactly your trades-per-day count. The daily-loss check compares the balance with the balance at the start of that day.
  • Profit targets, the daily loss limit, the max drawdown and minimum days are the plan's own verified values — including multi-phase targets (a plan showing "8% → 5%" must clear both phases, in order). Each phase starts fresh at the plan's account size.
  • Static drawdown: the floor never moves. Trailing drawdown: the floor follows your highest balance. EOD trailing: the floor only updates from end-of-day balances. A floor breach means the balance touches or falls below the floor.
  • Futures-style plans that state their drawdown (and target) in dollars use those dollar amounts directly.
  • Minimum trading days gate the final pass only — applied once to the whole attempt. Attempts still open after 250 trading days are counted separately, not as passes or failures.

Assumptions — read before trusting the number

  • Trades are independent — no winning or losing streaks beyond what randomness produces.
  • Every win pays exactly your average reward-to-risk and every loss costs exactly your risk % — fixed win/loss sizes, no partial exits, no variable sizing.
  • No slippage, spreads, commissions, swap or fees are deducted.
  • No news gaps, halts, weekend gaps or liquidity events — prices never jump over a floor.
  • No psychology: the simulated trader never revenge-trades, over-risks, hesitates or breaks the plan.
  • Consistency rules, time limits, news-trading bans and other plan rules beyond the ones modelled here are not enforced by this simulation.

FAQs

Is this simulator a prediction of whether I will pass?

No. It is a statistical estimate from a simplified random model: independent trades, fixed win and loss sizes, and none of the costs or psychology of real trading. Use it to see whether a plan's rules leave room for your numbers — not as a promise.

Where do the profit targets and drawdown rules come from?

From PIPVANT's verified plan data — the same snapshot behind the firm pages and the data ledger. If a plan's verified data lacks a profit target or a max drawdown, the simulator refuses to simulate it instead of inventing defaults.

Why do two runs give slightly different numbers?

Each run simulates 3,000 fresh attempts with new randomness, so results move by a point or so. That wobble is the honest size of the estimate's precision.

How does the simulator handle two-phase challenges?

It simulates the phases in order, using each phase's real target from the verified data. Each phase starts fresh at the plan's account size with its own drawdown floor, and the attempt fails if either phase breaches.
