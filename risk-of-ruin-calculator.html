How it works

Each simulation starts at a balance of 100. On every trade, a random draw decides win or loss using your win rate. A win multiplies the balance by (1 + risk% × average win in R); a loss multiplies it by (1 − risk% × average loss in R). The simulation stops early the moment the balance falls to or below 100 × (1 − ruin level%) — that run counts as ruined — or after 1,000 trades.

Ruin probability = ruined runs ÷ 5,000 runs. The formula per trade is: new balance = balance × (1 + (risk ÷ 100) × R), where R is +average win on a win and −average loss on a loss. Small changes in risk per trade change ruin odds far more than small changes in win rate — that is what the sweep table shows.

🎲 Win rate is not safety

A 60% win rate can still carry a real ruin probability if the average loss is much larger than the average win. Ruin is a property of the whole system, not one statistic.

📉 Risk % dominates

Doubling your risk per trade does not double your ruin probability — it can multiply it tenfold, because losses compound against a shrinking balance.

🛡️ Prop accounts ruin faster

On a challenge, your ruin level is the firm's max drawdown — often 8–10%. Set the ruin level to your firm's limit and size so the ruin probability stays near zero.

Risk of ruin FAQs

What is risk of ruin?

Risk of ruin is the probability that your balance falls by a set percentage — your ruin level — before you complete a given number of trades. It depends on your win rate, your average win and loss in R, and how much you risk per trade.

How does this calculator work?

It runs 5,000 Monte Carlo simulations of up to 1,000 trades each. Every trade wins with your win-rate probability and pays your average win in R, or loses your average loss in R, sized by your risk per trade. A run is ruined if the balance ever falls to or below the ruin level.

What ruin probability is acceptable?

There is no universal number, but most disciplined traders aim for a ruin probability as close to 0% as possible over their planning horizon. If the calculator shows double digits, the usual fix is smaller risk per trade — not a better entry.
