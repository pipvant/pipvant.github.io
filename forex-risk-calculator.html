Risk calculator FAQs

How do I calculate position size in forex?

Position size = (account size × risk %) ÷ stop loss in pips ÷ pip value. Example: a $10,000 account risking 1% ($100) with a 50-pip stop = $2 per pip = 0.20 standard lots. The calculator above does it instantly.

What is a good risk percentage per trade?

Most professionals risk 0.5–2% per trade. On prop firm challenges, 1% is the practical standard — it keeps you far from the drawdown limit while letting winners compound.

What is pip value?

For most USD-quoted pairs, one standard lot = $10 per pip. Mini lot = $1/pip, micro lot = $0.10/pip. Adjust the pip value field for JPY pairs or cross rates.

Is this calculator free?

Yes — free forever, no signup. It's part of PIPVANT's free toolkit: journal, prop firm matcher, risk calculator and economic calendar.

Are the instrument preset values exact for my broker?

Presets use standard contract specifications — for example $10 per pip per standard lot on major forex pairs, and 100 oz per standard gold lot. Contract sizes differ between brokers and CFD providers, so every preset value (pip/tick size and value) stays editable. Confirm the pip or tick value with your own broker before trading.

How does the prop firm plan preset work?

Pick a firm and a plan and the calculator loads that plan's real daily loss and max drawdown rules from PIPVANT's verified database. It then shows what share of those limits your chosen risk per trade would use — for example, at 1% risk on a plan with a 5% daily loss limit, one trade uses 20% of the daily limit.
