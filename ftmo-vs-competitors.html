Reading the table like a trader

Three things matter more than the fee gap. First, the daily loss limit: E8 Markets allows only 2.5% daily on a $100K plan versus FTMO's 5% — tighter room means more ways to breach. Second, the profit target: firms asking for 10% (FTMO, Alpha) demand more than those asking for 8% (The5ers, E8, Funding Pips) — roughly 25% more distance for the same fee bracket. Third, where the split starts: The5ers begins at 50% and scales to 100% as you grow, while FTMO starts at 80–90% — a short-term trader keeps more at FTMO, a long-term scaler can end up keeping more at The5ers. None of this says which firm is "better" — it says which terms fit your trading style.

FTMO comparison FAQs

Is FTMO the cheapest prop firm?

No. Per verified October 2026 data, FTMO's $100K challenge costs $619 one-time, while The5ers charges $455, E8 Markets $488 and Alpha Capital Group $497 for the same account size. Fee alone doesn't decide value — compare drawdown rules, profit targets and payout terms too.

Which firms use the same drawdown type as FTMO?

All five forex firms compared here use static drawdown based on the initial balance — the same model as FTMO. The percentages differ though: E8 Markets has the tightest daily limit (2.5%), while the others allow 4–5% daily with 8–10% maximum. Check each firm's official site for exact rules.

What profit split do FTMO and its competitors offer?

FTMO: 80% (2-Step) or 90% (1-Step). E8 Markets: 80–100% by program. Funding Pips: 80–100% by payout plan. Alpha Capital Group: 80–90% with an add-on. FundedNext: 80–95% with scale-up. The5ers: 50% scaling up to 100% with account growth. Confirm exact terms for your plan on each firm's official site.

Why are futures firms not in this comparison?

FTMO is a forex/CFD prop firm, so this page compares it only against other forex prop firms for a like-for-like comparison. Futures firms (Apex, Topstep, TakeProfitTrader, MyFundedFutures) use different instruments, rules and pricing models — they have their own page: Futures Prop Firms Compared.
